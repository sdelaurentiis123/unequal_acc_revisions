#7 + #8 (LISA prediction tone-down — your main concern). This is the
   change that needs the most attention from you. The §4.2 closing block
   is rewritten with three paragraphs. The headline:

     - The bump claim is kept, with a stronger physical justification:
       Peters' formulas conserve q at leading order during inspiral, so
       the q ≈ 0.99 plateau established in the gas phase is preserved
       through inspiral, not driven back to 1.

     - I verified this by extending the integration of Eq. 12 from
       a₀ = 10³ R_S all the way to a = 5 R_S (well into the LISA band).
       q stays at ≈0.99 even as e is circularized to ≈0 by GW. The
       integration code is in scripts/run_q_evolution_to_LISA.py.

     - Two new caveats stated explicitly in the prose: (i) Magda's
       lookup is gridded at Δq_b = 0.1, so we don't have direct
       simulation data in q ∈ (0.95, 1.0) — the regime our binary
       lives in during inspiral; (ii) the cavity reorientation
       timescale, which I claimed in §3.2 proceeds on the apsidal
       precession timescale, hasn't been quantitatively compared to
       the gas q-evolution timescale.

     - Three open questions stated explicitly (these are your concerns
       from #7): population fraction passing through (e=0.2, q=1);
       LISA's 0.5% precision floor relative to Δq ~ 0.01; the
       ~100 expected MBHB events.
